# Product ownership: scope and boundaries

## What a Product Owner does (in one paragraph)

The Product Owner decides **what gets built, in what order, and why**. They turn business goals into requirements, keep a prioritised to-do list (the *backlog*) for the engineering teams, and make sure each two-week *sprint* delivers something useful.

## What the role covered

| Product | Capabilities in scope |
|---|---|
| **Concierge** | Experience catalog · reservations and seating · personalization · transportation information |
| **Shop** | Product and service catalog · order and transaction management · inventory availability |
| **Dine** | Menu and recipe management · food and beverage ordering · food inventory · dietary and taste preferences |

Together: **90+ features across 14 capabilities**.

## How the work ran

- **Prioritisation.** Features were sequenced across three milestones (Pre-Cruise, On-Board, Launch Preparation) so the highest-value guest journeys shipped first.
- **Integration.** The commerce platform connected to 20+ systems, including payments, reservations, inventory, seating, fraud checks and crew task management.
- **Cadence.** Two-week sprints with Jira and Confluence, and Selenium for test automation.
- **Stakeholders.** Work spanned the cruise company's leaders from CXO to general-manager level, plus design, platform and engineering partners.
- **Traceability.** Every requirement traced back to a business goal in the requirements document, so scope debates had a reference point.

## What sat with other teams

Being clear about boundaries matters as much as the wins.

| Area | Owner |
|---|---|
| Wearable payments (card handling, settlement, reconciliation) | A separate product team; commerce integrated with it |
| Online check-in and embarkation | A separate product team |
| Sensors, the wearable hardware and the IoT network | Platform and hardware teams |
| The recommendation engine | Part of the wider personalization programme; not yet in production in public reporting on the programme |
